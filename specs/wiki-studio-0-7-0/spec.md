## Why

0.6.0 made wiki-studio's own writers safe; it did not make the curation loop
trustworthy, and it says so. Three gaps remain. Mixed-writer safety is not
claimed: memkernel's bridge takes a whole-wiki snapshot before an export and
restores it outside the wiki lock on failure, which can rewrite another
writer's committed pages and delete its archived `plan.json`. Nothing says
which knowledge is approved: a page's `status` is whatever its author wrote,
memkernel imports drafts, and no record binds an approval to a revision, so a
manual edit after review is indistinguishable from the reviewed text. Nothing
says which contract a wiki follows: `info` prints paths, not versions or
capabilities, a project-local schema copy cannot be told apart from the
packaged one, and an adapter cannot check compatibility before it writes.

The cost is the roadmap's central claim, "replacing a revision keeps the
obsolete version from returning as current knowledge", which cannot be shown
today. This release is wiki-studio's half of Release B: it proves the bridge
path, publishes a versioned contract, names the page and the actor, records
approval as a fact about one revision under one policy, marks replacement
explicitly, and reports what needs attention without a model. Release B
closes only when the joint correction loop passes on clean installs with
memkernel's counterpart (memkernel #48); 0.7.0 may ship before that and then
says so.

## User Scenarios

### US1: The bridge path is proven (Priority: HIGH)

**Given** a wiki and a proposals directory at `<root>/.memkernel/wiki-proposals`, and a test that replays memkernel's bridge calls exactly (memkernel-shaped `create` edits, one `append-index`, one `append-log`, `api.propose` then `api.promote`) without importing memkernel
**When** the export runs alone, runs twice, is interrupted after its commit, and runs while a CLI `promote` of another proposal lands
**Then** the pages, index and log lines land on current content with a `committed` receipt and a byte-identical archive; a second export of the same page is refused with nothing written; the interrupted promote resolves from its receipt without re-applying anything; both concurrent writers' work lands and neither is reverted; and a copied reproduction of memkernel's snapshot shows that its unlocked restore rewrites the other writer's page and deletes its archived `plan.json`, while the same failing export without the snapshot leaves that work intact

### US2: A wiki declares its contract (Priority: HIGH)

**Given** a wiki initialised by `wiki-studio init`, a wiki made before 0.7.0, and a wiki whose manifest names a newer `manifest_version` or `schema.version`
**When** a host calls `api.info` (or `info --json`) and then `propose`, `promote` or `approve`
**Then** the initialised wiki has a root manifest with a stable `wiki_id`, the schema identity and version and a fingerprint of its loaded project overrides; `info` reports the package version, schema identity, declared and current fingerprint, format versions and the capabilities of the shipped build; the pre-0.7.0 wiki behaves as in 0.6.0 except the three integrity refusals of FR-011 and `info` says how to add a manifest; the newer manifest is refused with exit 4 naming both versions; on a governed wiki a changed override is refused with exit 4 until `init --update-schema`, and on a managed wiki it is a warning

### US3: Pages and actors are named (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Given** an agent that creates a page with `propose --from` or `ingest`, the memkernel bridge that creates pages through `api.propose`, and a curator who passes `--by` or sets `WIKI_STUDIO_ACTOR`
**When** the proposals are written and promoted
**Then** the CLI and ingest creates carry a fresh `page_id: pg-<32 hex>` that the preview, commit and any approval cover, the bridge's bytes land unchanged unless it opts in, lint refuses a malformed or duplicated `page_id`, and `plan.json` and the receipt record who proposed and promoted as `{name, source}` with `source` `flag`, `env` or `none`, never the OS user

### US4: Approval is a fact about one revision under one policy (Priority: HIGH)

**Given** a managed or governed wiki with a draft page, a page that declares `status: stable` with no record, a proposal whose frontmatter carries `approved_by:`, and an approved page that is later edited by hand
**When** a curator runs `wiki-studio approve`, a host calls `api.review_state`, and the manifest's policy is edited
**Then** `approve` refuses the draft (exit 9) and auto-approval skips it; the stable page with no record is `unapproved`; the proposal is refused (exit 4) naming the key; the edited page is `stale` until its new bytes are approved, then `approved` again; a policy edit makes approved pages `policy-changed`; and every decision is one immutable record under `.audit/approvals/` written only by `promoter.py` under the lock

### US5: Replacement is explicit (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Given** a page that carries `superseded_by: <wiki path>`, a target that is missing, the page itself, or superseded in turn, and a page carrying memkernel's `corrects:` key with several references
**When** lint, `approve` and `review_state` run, and the page is promoted
**Then** lint reports the missing, self, chained and cyclic targets; `approve` refuses the superseded page; `review_state` reports it `superseded` whatever its records say; and the `corrects` line is preserved byte for byte, registered by ADR-0002 as memkernel-interpreted with its syntax not final

### US6: Findings without a model, and release 0.7.0 (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Given** a wiki with unapproved, stale, policy-changed and superseded-but-indexed pages and deterministic health findings
**When** a host calls `api.findings` or runs `wiki-studio findings --json` at session start
**Then** it gets counts by kind first and a versioned list of findings, exit 0, from code that cannot take a backend; and 0.7.0 ships with its CHANGELOG, a compatibility matrix, and the memkernel limitation kept until a tested memkernel release without the snapshot is listed

## Requirements

### US1 — the combined integration gate (test-only; no API change)

- **FR-001 (bridge-shaped contract tests)**: `tests/test_bridge_contract.py` MUST replay the call sequence of memkernel's `propose_and_promote` (memkernel `src/memkernel/wiki/studio.py:151-193`) without importing memkernel: `create` edits with memkernel-shaped pages (`status: draft`, the extra keys `memkernel_ref` and `memory_type`, a `memkernel://<project>/<ref>` source), one `append-index` and one `append-log`, through `api.propose(edits, paths, name="memkernel-export-<ts>-<hex>", source="memkernel wiki_export", backend="memkernel")` then `api.promote(proposal, paths)`, with `paths = WikiPaths.for_root(root, wiki_dir=..., proposals_dir=<root>/.memkernel/wiki-proposals)`. It MUST assert: (a) the pages land, and the index and log lines land on current content; (b) `<proposals>/.applied/<name>/receipt.json` has `state: "committed"` and carries the submission digest, and the archived `plan.json` and `preview/` are byte-identical to the submission; (c) a second export of the same page is refused as a `create` of an existing page with nothing written: when the second export is proposed after the first has landed, `api.propose` refuses it (`ContractViolationError`, exit 4, today's `validate_page_edit_semantics` rule), and when it was proposed before the first landed, `promote` refuses it (exit 9 through the CLI, `PromoteValidationError` through the API); (d) a promote interrupted after its commit (receipt left in the proposal directory) resolves from the receipt on retry and re-applies nothing; (e) the lock file sits beside the wiki, never inside it, and the wiki lints clean afterwards.
- **FR-002 (combined concurrency test)**: A test MUST run a wiki-studio `promote` of another proposal as a subprocess through the CLI (started through `tests/helpers/promote_bootstrap.py`) while a bridge-shaped export is inside `api.promote`. Both MUST land, neither MAY be reverted, and both archives MUST exist.
- **FR-003 (reproduction of the snapshot hazard)**: `tests/helpers/memkernel_snapshot.py` MUST copy `_WikiSnapshot.take` and `restore` from memkernel `src/memkernel/wiki/sync.py:299-363`, citing the source file, lines and the memkernel revision in its header, as 0.5.0 copied memkernel's quoting rules. A test MUST wrap a failing bridge-shaped export in that copy, with a second writer committing a proposal between `take` and `restore`, and show the second writer's page rewritten and its wiki-side archived `plan.json` (`.audit/proposals/<name>/plan.json`) deleted; the same failing export without the wrapper MUST leave the second writer's work intact. The test is evidence for the closure, not a test of memkernel.
- **FR-004 (clean-install harness)**: `tests/integration/` MUST hold tests that are skipped unless `memkernel` is importable. A CI job in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, separate from the coverage job, MUST build the wiki-studio wheel, install it with memkernel at a pinned release or git ref (named in the workflow and in `docs/compatibility.md`) into a fresh virtual environment, and run the real `WikiExporter` and `WikiImporter` against it. It is two jobs: `memkernel-compat` (export, re-export and import of creates and appends against the pinned memkernel) starts non-blocking and becomes blocking as soon as it is green on main; `memkernel-loop` (the four Release B loop cases) is named and labelled as expected to fail until memkernel's counterpart (memkernel #48: snapshot removal and approved-only import) is merged, stays non-blocking until then, and becomes blocking for the 0.7.0 tag only after that merge (FR-039). memkernel MUST NOT become a dependency of the package or of the default dev group, and the suite MUST NOT mock memkernel's `MemoryService`.

The CHANGELOG Limitations item about memkernel's outer snapshot is replaced only when `docs/compatibility.md` lists a tested memkernel release without the snapshot; the CHANGELOG MUST NOT state what memkernel's next release does.

### US2 — the versioned knowledge contract

- **FR-006 (root manifest)**: `init` (CLI and `api.init`) MUST write `<wiki>/.wiki-studio.json` from `promoter.py`, by exclusive creation (`O_CREAT | O_EXCL | O_NOFOLLOW`), never overwriting, after the containment check: a symlink at that path is refused and its target untouched; the wiki directory's configured spelling may be a symlink alias, as Release A's FR-002 requires, and is resolved once, after which every component below it is reached descriptor-relative with `O_NOFOLLOW` (owner decision on the PR #47 review, 2026-10-26). Every read of the manifest MUST pass the same containment check and open it with `O_NOFOLLOW`. Keys: `manifest_version: 1`; `wiki_id` (a random UUID, the wiki's identity across clones and moves; the receipt's device and inode stay for recovery only); `schema: {"id": "wiki-studio/page-schema", "version": <n>}` (version 1 is the 0.6.0 schema; version 2, which adds the optional keys of FR-014 and FR-033, is set by FR-019); `schema_fingerprint` (FR-008); `policy` (`approval_required`, `approver_required`, `auto_approve_on_promote`, each `false` by default; semantics in FR-020); `created_by: {"package", "version"}`. `schema_source` is derived at read time (FR-007) and never persisted. `init` on an existing wiki without a manifest adds one and touches nothing else; on a wiki with a manifest it changes nothing. A malformed manifest (not JSON, a missing key, a wrong type) is a contract violation (exit 4) naming the key. Vocabulary, used everywhere in this spec and in the docs: a **governed** wiki has a manifest whose policy sets `approval_required: true`; a **managed** wiki has a manifest with the default policy; a **legacy** wiki has no manifest. `InitResult` gains `manifest: str` (`created`, `existing` or `updated`) as a last field with a default.
- **FR-007 (schema identity and the one schema loader)**: The packaged rules are identified by `schema.version`, which only a decision record (ADR) bumps; a package upgrade that keeps the version changes nothing a wiki must react to. One shared loader MUST replace the body of `prompt.load_schema_files` (`prompt.py:36-46`, which today follows a symlink through `is_file()` and `read_text()`): for each of `ingest-rules.md`, `page-types.md` and `citation-rules.md` under `<wiki>/schema/` it passes the candidate through the containment check and opens it with `O_NOFOLLOW`, refusing (exit 4) a symlinked override or schema directory with its target unread, and returns the exact override bytes it read beside the merged texts. Those bytes feed both the prompt and the fingerprint, so what is hashed is what the model was shown. `load_schema_files(paths)` keeps its name, signature and return value (the CI wheel smoke test imports it) and delegates to the loader. `schema_source` is `project` when any of the three overrides exists and `packaged` otherwise.
- **FR-008 (fingerprint recipe)**: `schema_fingerprint` MUST be the lowercase hex SHA-256 over the loaded overrides that are present, sorted by file name, each fed as the UTF-8 file name (`citation-rules.md`, `ingest-rules.md`, `page-types.md`), one NUL byte, then the raw file bytes. With no override it is the SHA-256 of empty input (`e3b0c442…b855`), never an empty string. Packaged files and other files under `<wiki>/schema/` (for example `lint-rules.md`) never enter it. Raw bytes are hashed without normalisation; a line-ending change is a schema change for this purpose. The recipe is documented in `docs/knowledge-contract.md`.
- **FR-009 (drift)**: The manifest stores the fingerprint declared at `init`. Drift is a mismatch between that and the current fingerprint. `propose` (CLI and `api.propose`), `promote` (CLI and `api.promote`, dry run included) and `approve` (CLI and `api.approve`) MUST refuse on drift with `ContractViolationError` (exit 4) naming the declared and the current fingerprint and the remedy `init --update-schema`, only on a governed wiki; on a managed wiki the CLI verbs MUST print the warning to stderr and the API MUST return it in the result's `warnings` (`PromoteResult.warnings`, `ApproveResult.warnings`). `api.propose` keeps its `Path` return and MUST emit the same message immediately as a `DriftWarning` (a `UserWarning` subclass in `wiki_studio.errors`) through `warnings.warn` at the point of the check, so a caller that only creates a proposal is warned at once and can escalate it to an error with the standard warnings filter; `api.info` and the following `api.promote`'s `warnings` carry the same message, and the CLI `propose` prints it to stderr. `info` and `api.info` MUST report the declared and the current fingerprint and whether they differ; a host checks `api.info` before it writes, and the exit-4 refusal is the backstop. A legacy wiki has nothing to drift from. `init --update-schema` (`api.init(..., update_schema=True)`) MUST rewrite only `schema_fingerprint`, by writing the whole manifest to a temporary sibling (exclusive, no-follow), fsyncing it and replacing the manifest atomically under the wiki lock, never in place. An ordinary package upgrade never trips the check.
- **FR-010 (`api.info` and `info --json`)**: A new `api.info(paths) -> InfoResult` MUST report: `package_version`; `schema_contract` (`id`, `version`, `source`, `fingerprint` current, `declared_fingerprint` or `null`, `drift` boolean); `formats` (`patch_set: 1`, `receipt: 1`, `ingest_log: 1`, then `approval: 1` and `findings: 1` when those scopes ship); `capabilities` (stable strings from `wiki-lock`, `receipts`, `base-revision`, `root-manifest`, `page-id`, `attribution`, `approval-record`, `superseded-by`, `findings`); and `manifest` (`state` `present` or `missing`; when present, `manifest_version`, `wiki_id`, `policy` and `mode` `governed` or `managed`; when missing, `mode` `legacy` and the one-line instruction to run `wiki-studio init`). `info --json` MUST print every existing key with its 0.6.0 name and meaning (the string key `schema` and `schema_dir` included) plus these keys; the text form prints them too. Both lists describe the shipped build: a scope the circuit breaker cuts has no capability string and no format entry, and a test MUST pin that every advertised capability resolves to a verb, API function or key that exists in the build.
- **FR-011 (compatibility check)**: `propose`, `promote`, `approve` and `init` MUST read the manifest; a `manifest_version` or `schema.version` newer than this package supports is a contract violation (exit 4) naming both versions and the upgrade; a `schema.id` other than the identities this package supports (`wiki-studio/page-schema`) is a contract violation (exit 4) naming the id, independently of the version. A missing manifest means a legacy wiki: every 0.6.0 verb works as in 0.6.0 except three integrity refusals that apply to every wiki, legacy included: a symlinked project schema override (FR-007), an edit whose normalised path is reserved (FR-028) and a self-declared approval key in frontmatter (FR-029). The governed operations need a manifest: `approve` and auto-approval refuse or skip without one (`approve` exits 4 naming `init`), because a record needs `wiki_id` and a policy hash, and `review_state` reports every page of a legacy wiki as `unapproved` with reason `manifest_missing`.
- **FR-012 (compatibility matrix)**: `docs/compatibility.md` MUST list each tested wiki-studio × memkernel pair, the evidence that covers it (the FR-004 harness run, with the CI run named), and the capabilities each side needs; and state how to re-create a pre-0.6.0 proposal whose `update` edits lack revision preconditions (`read --json`, then `propose` again).
- **FR-013 (contract tests)**: Tests MUST pin that `init` creates the manifest once and never overwrites it; a symlinked manifest path is refused with its target untouched, and an alias spelling of the wiki directory reaches the real wiki; the fingerprint changes when one byte of a loaded override changes, is independent of directory listing order, ignores packaged files and unloaded files, and equals the SHA-256 of empty input without overrides; an override symlink pointing outside the wiki is refused (exit 4) with its target unread; a package upgrade alone (a changed packaged schema document) never trips the drift check; `init --update-schema` after adding the first override and after removing the last one leaves `info` reporting the derived source and no drift; drift is exit 4 on a governed wiki and a warning on a managed one through `propose` (stderr, and `DriftWarning` from `api.propose`) and `promote` (stderr and `PromoteResult.warnings`) in US2, and through `approve` in US4 (task 32: managed drift in `ApproveResult.warnings` and on stderr, governed drift exit 4, a newer manifest exit 4, each through the CLI and `api.approve`); `info --json` pins the new keys and keeps the old ones; a newer manifest is refused with exit 4; a manifest with an unknown `schema.id` and a supported version is refused with exit 4; a wiki without a manifest behaves as in 0.6.0.

### US3 — identity and attribution

- **FR-014 (`page_id`)**: An optional frontmatter key `page_id: pg-<32 lowercase hex>`, a single-line scalar (the form ADR-0001 found survives memkernel's parse and render). `api.propose` MUST gain `assign_page_ids: bool = False` (keyword-only; the complete signature is under FR-017): when true, a `create` edit whose content has no `page_id` gets `pg-` + `uuid4().hex` inserted as the last frontmatter line before the closing `---`, before the preview is written, so the preview, the commit and any approval cover the same bytes. The CLI `propose --from` passes `assign_page_ids=True`, and multi-page `ingest` (CLI and `api.ingest`) assigns the same way on its creates; with the default, `api.propose` never changes a caller's bytes, so the memkernel bridge's creates land byte for byte until it opts in. A caller-supplied `page_id` is never rewritten. `update`, append and legacy single-source paths are unchanged; existing pages gain a `page_id` only through an ordinary guarded `update`.
- **FR-015 (`page_id` lint)**: The structural linter MUST report a `page_id` that does not match `^pg-[0-9a-f]{32}$` and every page sharing a `page_id` with another page. Because promote lints the staged tree, this also refuses a `create` that copies an existing id (exit 9).
- **FR-016 (page key)**: A page's key is its `page_id` when it has a well-formed one, else its canonical wiki-relative path (FR-023's canonical form). Approval records, `review_state` and findings use that key. Lint already ties `slug` to the file stem and the page type to the directory, so any rename changes the bytes and the page goes `stale`; what `page_id` buys is only that after a rename `review_state` reports `stale` with the earlier record and its approved revision instead of `unapproved`, and memkernel can keep one memory identity across the move.
- **FR-017 (actor attribution)**: `--by NAME` on `propose`, `promote` and `approve`, and the environment variable `WIKI_STUDIO_ACTOR`, MUST resolve one actor `{name, source}`: an explicit `--by` (or the API keyword `by=`) is `source: "flag"`, else a non-empty `WIKI_STUDIO_ACTOR` is `source: "env"`, else `{name: null, source: "none"}`. The OS user name is never captured implicitly. The complete signatures, stated once: `api.propose(edits, paths, *, name, source="", rationale="", backend=PROPOSE_BACKEND, by=None, assign_page_ids=False) -> Path`; `api.promote` gains `by: str | None = None` beside its existing keyword-only options; `api.approve(pages, paths, *, by=None, expected=None) -> ApproveResult` (FR-021). Every new parameter is keyword-only with a default, so its position carries no compatibility meaning. The actor is recorded as optional keys: `plan.json` `proposer` (written by `propose`; `WikiPatchSet` gains `proposer` as a last field with a default), receipt `actor` (receipt version stays 1, the key documented as a minor-version addition like `base_sha256` in 0.6.0), and the approval record's `approver` (FR-024).
- **FR-018 (attribution is not authentication)**: README, `SECURITY.md` and `docs/patch-set-format.md` MUST state that `--by` is attribution, not authentication; the host supplies authority; local audit files provide traceability, not tamper-proof evidence. `docs/patch-set-format.md` documents `proposer`, `actor` and `page_id` assignment.
- **FR-019 (ADR-0002 and schema version 2)**: `docs/adr/0002-page-identity-and-review-keys.md`, in ADR-0001's form, MUST record in one decision: `page_id` (FR-014..FR-016); the reserved approval keys (FR-029); `superseded_by` (FR-033..FR-035); the memkernel-owned `corrects` key registered as memkernel-interpreted with its value syntax marked not final (FR-036); that review state describes the wiki revision and its mapping to memkernel's tiers, withdrawal and `Memory.superseded_by` is memkernel's decision; and the bump of `schema.version` to 2. The packaged schema constant moves to 2 in the same phase, citing the ADR, and `schema/page-types.md` documents the optional keys.

### US4 — approval bound to the revision and the policy

- **FR-020 (policy)**: The manifest's `policy` has exactly three boolean keys: `approval_required` (the wiki is governed), `approver_required` (`approve` refuses without an actor name), `auto_approve_on_promote` (a successful promote also approves each content page it wrote, except drafts, under the promoter's actor; FR-030). `policy_sha256` MUST be the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the canonical JSON (sorted keys, separators `,` and `:`, no whitespace, UTF-8) of `{"approver_required": <bool>}` only, the one key that changes what an approval accepts; `approval_required` decides whether approval is enforced, not what it accepts, so turning a managed wiki governed keeps every existing approval `approved` (owner decision, 2026-10-26, with a test), and toggling `auto_approve_on_promote` never changes the hash either. The host edits the policy; a proposal cannot (FR-028). Approvals attest page bytes under the approver rule, not the project's schema overrides: a record does not store the schema fingerprint, and an acknowledged schema change (`init --update-schema`) leaves every existing approval as it was (owner decision, 2026-10-26). The change stays visible in `info`, and structural lint re-checks every page at `promote` and `approve`. The docs say so in these words: `approved` means this revision was accepted under the approver rule; it does not mean the page is valid against the latest schema, and a test changes an override, acknowledges it, and asserts an approved page stays `approved`.
- **FR-021 (`approve`)**: `wiki-studio approve <page>... [--revision SHA] [--by NAME]` and `api.approve(pages, paths, *, by=None, expected=None) -> ApproveResult` (`pages` wiki-relative paths; `expected` a mapping from wiki path to the sha256 the caller read, keys normalised like page paths). `--revision` is accepted only with exactly one page (exit 4 otherwise) and maps to `expected`. It runs in `promoter.py` under the wiki lock: (1) read each live page once through the wiki boundary and hash it (a path outside the wiki or through a symlink exits 7 and a missing page exits 5, as `read_page`); (2) preflight the whole batch and refuse with `PromoteValidationError` (exit 9, nothing written), naming each page and reason, when for any page an expected revision differs from the live hash, the structural linter reports a violation for the page, `status` is `draft`, the page carries `superseded_by`, or the policy requires an approver and the actor has no name; (3) for each page, if a verified record for its live sha256 already satisfies the current policy (under any policy hash, as `review_state` judges it in FR-026), report the page in `existing` and write nothing, since a new record would add nothing; otherwise write one record under the current `policy_sha256` (FR-023..FR-025); then release the lock. A legacy wiki is refused with exit 4 naming `init` (FR-011); a newer manifest and drift on a governed wiki are exit 4 (FR-009, FR-011). The explicit `approve` accepts any page the structural linter lists. `ApproveResult` carries `published` (record paths written), `existing` (tuples already recorded, reported as no-ops) and `warnings`.
- **FR-022 (batch publication semantics)**: Validation is atomic (step 2 writes nothing); publication is not: an I/O failure after some records are published leaves them in place, since each is independent and immutable, and the error (`OutputWriteError`, exit 6) names the pages published and the pages not. Running `approve` again is idempotent: the published tuples are reported as existing, the rest are written. The docs state this.
- **FR-023 (record layout and the canonical path digest)**: A decision is one file: `<wiki>/.audit/approvals/id/<page_id>/<sha256>/<policy_sha256>/<decision>.json` for a page with a `page_id`, and `<wiki>/.audit/approvals/path/<path-digest>/<sha256>/<policy_sha256>/<decision>.json` for one without, with `<decision>` `approved`. `<path-digest>` is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the canonical wiki-relative path encoded as UTF-8, where the canonical path is the edit path normalised with `posixpath.normpath`, with forward slashes and no leading `./` or `/` (so `./concepts/x.md` and `concepts/x.md` are one page). The identity of a decision is the tuple (page key, revision, policy, decision) and every element is in the path, so the same bytes approved under a changed policy get their own record and never collide with or overwrite the earlier one. The two namespaces keep a path-derived key from ever sharing a directory with a `page_id`. The recipe is documented in `docs/knowledge-contract.md`. Tests MUST cover a collision case (`concepts/x.md` beside a root page named `concepts__x.md`, and a `page_id` equal to a path-derived name) and an equivalence case (`concepts/x.md` and `./concepts/x.md` locate the same records).
- **FR-024 (record content and verification)**: A record is JSON with `approval_version: 1`, `wiki_id`, `page_key` (the `page_id` or the canonical path), `path`, `sha256`, `policy_sha256`, `decision: "approved"`, `proposal` (`{name, submission_digest}` when the revision came through a receipt that wrote these bytes: for auto-approval the promoting receipt, for an explicit `approve` the newest committed receipt under `<proposals_dir>/.applied/` whose tracked write gives this path the live sha256 as its post-commit hash, best effort: only receipts under the caller's own proposals directory are searched, so a CLI `approve` does not find receipts another writer archived elsewhere, such as the bridge's `.memkernel/wiki-proposals`; `null` otherwise), `proposer`, `approver`, `promoter` (each `{name, source}` or `null`), and `timestamp` (UTC, ISO 8601). Every read MUST verify, before a record counts, that it is a regular file inside the boundary, parses, has `approval_version: 1`, the manifest's `wiki_id`, a `page_key` equal to the key being looked up (the `page_id` for a record under `id/`, the canonical path for a record under `path/`), and `sha256`, `policy_sha256` and `decision` equal to its directory and file names; a record that fails is skipped and reported in the result's warnings, so an enumeration can never attribute another page's history to the current page.
- **FR-025 (containment and exclusive publication)**: Every component of the approval tree MUST be created, enumerated and read inside the wiki boundary: the mapped names pass the containment check against `.audit/approvals/` before anything is created; directories are created without following a symlink; records are opened with `O_NOFOLLOW`; an enumeration skips and reports any symlink. `approve` refuses (exit 4) when a component it must create or read is a symlink, with the symlink's target untouched. A record is written to a temporary name in its directory, fsynced, and published by an exclusive link or create that never replaces an existing file (never a plain `rename`, which replaces on POSIX); a record that already exists for the exact tuple is the no-op, never rewritten. A test MUST plant a symlink at each level under `.audit/approvals/` pointing outside the wiki and show it refused with its target untouched.
- **FR-026 (`review_state`)**: `api.review_state(paths, *, pages=None) -> list[ReviewState]` MUST read without the lock (advisory and point-in-time, like `promote --dry-run`). `pages=None` covers every page the structural linter lists except the root `index.md` and `log.md`; an explicit list covers exactly those pages. Each `ReviewState` carries `path`, `page_key`, `state`, `reason`, `live_sha256`, `approved_sha256`, `record_path` and `warnings`. States, decided in this order: on a legacy wiki, `unapproved` with reason `manifest_missing`; `superseded` when the page carries `superseded_by` (FR-035); `unapproved` with reason `draft` for every page whose live `status` is `draft`; `approved` when a verified record for the live sha256 **satisfies the current policy**. Lookup therefore enumerates every `<policy_sha256>/` directory under the page key and the live sha256, verifies each record (FR-024), and judges it against the current policy, not by hash equality. A record satisfies the policy when `approver_required` is false (any verified record) or when it is true and the record's `approver` has a non-null `name`; so relaxing the policy never reopens approvals made under the stricter rule; `policy-changed` when records exist for the live sha256 but none satisfies the current policy (the tightening case: `approver_required` turned on and no record names an approver); `stale` when a verified record exists for the page but not for its live bytes (the manual-edit and update case); else `unapproved`. Lookup searches `id/<page_id>/` first, when the page has one, then `path/<digest>/` (records written before the page had an id), and reports the newest matching record by `timestamp`, with its approved sha256 and record path, so an importer can compare the bytes it reads. Frontmatter never makes a page approved: `status: stable` alone is `unapproved`.
- **FR-027 (a path reused after deletion)**: The `path/` namespace is keyed by the canonical path digest, so a page created later at a path that a deleted page once had can see the deleted page's records. wiki-studio has no delete action and cannot observe a deletion, so a path-keyed approval is a fact about **this path and these exact bytes**, not about a page's history (owner decision, 2026-10-26): a new page with different bytes at the same path reads at most `stale`, never `approved`; a new page with byte-identical content is indistinguishable from the old one and keeps its approval. Recreation safety comes from `page_id`: a create receives a fresh `page_id` (FR-014), so its lookup is `id/<new page_id>/` first and its bytes differ from the old page's (the `page_id` line differs), and it reads `unapproved`. The docs and the upgrade notes state this. Tests MUST cover all three: a deleted path-keyed page recreated with different bytes reads `stale` naming the old record, never `approved`; recreated with identical bytes reads `approved` (pinned as the documented behaviour); and a recreated page that received a fresh `page_id` reads `unapproved`.
- **FR-028 (reserved paths)**: `propose` (CLI and `api.propose`) and `promote` (CLI and `api.promote`, before staging) MUST refuse with `ContractViolationError` (exit 4, naming the path) any edit whose normalised path (`posixpath.normpath`, as `promoter.py` already applies) has a dot-prefixed component or lies under `schema/`, after the existing escape checks, which keep their exit codes. Tests MUST cover the manifest (`.wiki-studio.json`), an approval record path and a schema override (`schema/page-types.md`), and MUST show that a raw `./index.md` append still passes, as `docs/patch-set-format.md` documents.
- **FR-029 (self-declared approval)**: `propose` and `promote` MUST refuse with `ContractViolationError` (exit 4, naming the key and the page) a `create` or `update` whose frontmatter carries any of the keys reserved for the gate's record: `approved`, `approved_by`, `approval`, `review_state`. A search on 25 October 2026 found none of these keys in the frontmatter of `tests/fixtures/` or of code-copilot-team's `knowledge/wiki/`, so the list is final. `status: stable` stays allowed as a lifecycle label and never makes a page `approved`.
- **FR-030 (auto-approval)**: With `auto_approve_on_promote`, approval MUST be a separate operation after the promotion has completed (receipt `committed`, archive published), outside the receipt protocol: its records are not planned in the `committing` receipt, not tracked, and never affect recovery. `promote` takes the wiki lock again (the lock is not reentrant and was released) and, under one acquisition, checks each content page the promotion created or updated (never the `index.md` and `log.md` appends) against the receipt's post-commit sha256: a page that still matches and passes `approve`'s per-page conditions (not draft, not superseded, an approver name when required) gets a record with `approver` and `promoter` the promoter's actor, `proposer` from `plan.json` and `proposal` from the receipt; a page whose bytes no longer match gets no record and no promoter attribution, and a warning naming it. One intervening edit never blocks the other pages. A failure there is never a failed promote: the promotion stays committed, records already published remain, each failed, skipped or mismatched page gets a warning in `PromoteResult.warnings`, and its review state derives from the records that exist. A crash between records is recovered by running `approve` again. `PromoteResult` gains `approved_paths: list[str]` as a last field with a default. An interleaving test over a multi-page promotion (a second writer updating one page between the archive and the auto-approval) MUST pin that no record is written for the intervening revision under the promoter's identity while the unchanged pages are approved.
- **FR-031 (audit lint)**: The audit lint MUST admit `approvals/` in `.audit/`'s closed top-level set and validate the tree: only `id/` and `path/` at its top; `path/` names 64 lowercase hex; `id/` names match the `page_id` form; revision and policy levels 64 lowercase hex; files named `<decision>.json` with a known decision; each record valid per FR-024 against its location; no symlink anywhere; stray temporary files reported.
- **FR-032 (acceptance-case tests)**: One test per acceptance case on this side: a draft is refused by `approve` and skipped by auto-approval; a page with `status: stable` and no record is `unapproved`, and a proposal carrying `approved_by:` is refused; an approved page edited by hand is `stale`, and approving its new bytes makes it `approved` again; turning `approver_required` on makes pages approved without a named approver `policy-changed` while those with one stay `approved`, and turning it off again leaves every one `approved`. The FR-004 harness gains the four Release B loop cases end to end (`tests/integration/test_release_b_loop.py`), together with the pitch's retry property (a promote interrupted after its commit resolves from the receipt, and an export or import run twice creates no duplicate pages, records or memories); they run only in the clean-install job, are never skipped or marked as expected failures there, and are what keeps that job non-blocking until memkernel's importer reads `review_state`.

### US5 — `superseded_by`

- **FR-033 (`superseded_by` key)**: An optional frontmatter key `superseded_by: <wiki-relative path>` on the replaced page, recorded by ADR-0002. It holds a path, not a `page_id`, so it resolves and lints without `page_id`. memkernel records its side in its own ADR; this repository supplies the shared text and does not write memkernel's file.
- **FR-034 (`superseded_by` lint)**: The structural linter MUST report a `superseded_by` whose normalised target does not exist, is not a page, is the page itself, or is itself superseded (no chains, so no cycles); an absolute or escaping target is a violation. A broken target after a hand rename is therefore a lint violation.
- **FR-035 (`superseded` state)**: `review_state` MUST report `superseded` for a page that carries `superseded_by`, whatever its records say (after only the `manifest_missing` rule), and `approve` refuses it (FR-021).
- **FR-036 (`corrects`)**: ADR-0002 MUST register memkernel's `corrects:` key (the `memkernel_ref` values a page corrects) as memkernel-interpreted, with its value syntax marked not final until memkernel defines a restricted reference-token grammar that excludes commas and ambiguous quoting; the comma-separated single line is the starting proposal. wiki-studio MUST preserve the key byte for byte through propose and promote and MUST NOT interpret it; until the grammar is agreed and recorded in ADR-0002 it checks nothing beyond the single-line scalar form every frontmatter key already has. A fixture `tests/fixtures/corrects/multi-corrections.md` with several corrections on one page MUST be parsed by `yaml_lite` here and by memkernel's page parser in the clean-install harness, which together pin the round trip.

### US6 — findings without a model call, and the release

- **FR-037 (`findings`)**: `api.findings(paths, *, pages=None) -> FindingsResult` and `wiki-studio findings [--json] [--paths ...]` MUST take no backend parameter and resolve no backend, so no code path reaches a model; a test MUST pin both (the signature, and a run with backend resolution patched to fail). A completed run exits 0 whatever it finds; `lint --health --strict` stays the CI gate.
- **FR-038 (findings format)**: The JSON form is `{"findings_version": 1, "counts": {<kind>: <n>}, "pages_checked": <n>, "findings": [...]}` so a host can report curation debt at session start from `counts` without parsing descriptions. Key order carries no meaning: consumers read keys by name, and tests compare parsed JSON, never property order. Each finding has `kind`, `severity`, `pages` and `description` (the shape memkernel's `HealthItem` mirrors). Kinds: the deterministic health kinds `stale-claim`, `weak-orphan`, `missing-cross-link`, `avoid-format` (the health checks run without a backend, so `contradiction` never appears); the review kinds `unapproved`, `approval-stale`, `approval-policy-changed` (from `review_state`, severity `warning`), `superseded-still-indexed` (an `index.md` entry links a page that carries `superseded_by`, severity `warning`), and `no-page-id` (a content page without `page_id`, severity `warning`; only when FR-014 ships). The format is documented in `docs/knowledge-contract.md`. If S6 is cut, hosts use `lint --health` without a backend and `review_state`.
- **FR-039 (release)**: Version 0.7.0 in `pyproject.toml`, `src/wiki_studio/__init__.py` and the CLI contract test; README banner pinned to `v0.7.0`; `SECURITY.md` supported versions; CHANGELOG `[0.7.0]` with Added (root manifest, `api.info` and the `info` keys, the fingerprint and drift rule, `page_id`, `--by` attribution, `approve`, `api.approve`, `api.review_state`, approval records, auto-approval, `superseded_by`, `findings`, the bridge-shaped and hazard tests, the clean-install harness, `docs/compatibility.md`), Changed (the reserved-path and reserved-key refusals; the shared contained schema loader refusing symlinked overrides; schema version 2), Upgrade notes (a wiki made before 0.7.0 is legacy and works as in 0.6.0 until `init` adds a manifest, except the three refusals that apply to every wiki: symlinked schema overrides, reserved edit paths, self-declared approval keys; turning a managed wiki governed keeps its approvals; a manifest is required to approve; `api.propose` never assigns `page_id` unless asked; how to turn a wiki governed), and Limitations (the memkernel snapshot item kept per FR-005 unless a tested release without it is listed; Release B is not declared closed until the joint loop passes; any scope cut by FR-040). The coverage gate stays `--cov-fail-under=92` in CI, `CLAUDE.md` and `automation.json`; `CLAUDE.md`'s layout lists the new modules and verbs. If memkernel's counterpart is merged at release time, the FR-004 job becomes blocking (no `continue-on-error`) and `release.yml` runs it before publishing; otherwise it stays non-blocking and the Limitations say so.
- **FR-040 (circuit breaker)**: The build MUST follow the pitch's cut order and never cut S1, S2 or S4. End of week 2: if S1 and S2 are not downhill, cut S6 (US6 keeps FR-039 only; a follow-up issue is recorded). End of week 4: if S4 is not downhill, also cut `page_id` (FR-014..FR-016's `page_id` parts, the `page-id` capability, the `no-page-id` kind and ADR-0002's `page_id` section); approvals and findings key by path; attribution stays; 0.7.0 may ship and Release B stays open until `page_id` lands. End of week 5: if FR-034's lint rules are not done, ship FR-033, FR-035 and FR-036 without the chain and cycle checks. End of week 5, memkernel side: if memkernel's snapshot removal and approved-only import are not merged, ship 0.7.0 with this side's scopes, keep the memkernel limitation and do not declare Release B closed. Never, to save time: derive approval from frontmatter, weaken or skip a gate or concurrency test, add a runtime dependency, a model call or an exit code, make `WikiLock` reentrant, write memkernel code from here, or ship a schema key without ADR-0002. A third review round on one scope is an escalation to the owner, not exhaustion. **How the checkpoints are applied:** the unattended run has no milestone pause (owner decision), so the week checkpoints are manual cycle gates outside the automation. The owner applies them from the hill chart and the driver's ledger at the review of the build PR, or earlier if the run parks; a cut is made then as a follow-up commit on the feature branch that removes the scope's code, capability, format entry and docs, never by the driver. The automation's own escalations stay as the driver defines them: a review breaker, an origin-gate failure or a cap parks the run for the owner.

## Constraints

- **Standard library only.** `hashlib`, `json`, `os`, `posixpath`, `uuid` and `pathlib` suffice; no new runtime dependency; memkernel never enters the package or the default dev group.
- **Contracts.** Exit codes 0 and 2..11 keep their meaning and 1 stays fatal lint; no new exit code (refusals map to 4 contract violation, 9 validation, 10 lock or recovery, plus the existing 5, 6 and 7). The patch-set format gains optional keys only (`proposer`); the receipt stays version 1 with the optional `actor`; `api` changes are additive with defaults; existing `info --json` keys keep their names and meaning.
- **Single writer.** `promoter.py` stays the only module that writes into the wiki directory, now including the root manifest and the approval records; the single-writer tests (`tests/test_audit.py`, `tests/test_e2e.py`) stay green.
- **No model call** in `findings`, `approve` or `review_state`.
- **No reentrant `WikiLock`.** Auto-approval releases and re-acquires.
- **Approval is never derived from frontmatter.** Only a verified record makes a page `approved`.
- **Out of scope (no-gos).** Hosted collaboration, a new runtime, a required vector store, mandatory semantic judges, prose auto-rewrite; crash-safe multi-file transactions (approval records follow 0.6.0's guarantee: validated, serialised, rolled back on failure, not crash-safe across files); changes to memkernel's code, ADRs or CI; authentication, signatures, hash chains or tamper-proof audit; a `move` or `delete` patch-set action; a policy language beyond three booleans; an append-only approval ledger; automatic `page_id` backfill; the two tested quickstarts (a follow-up once the loop passes).
- **Schema keys.** No schema key without ADR-0002 and a CHANGELOG entry; the page schema shared with memkernel changes only through that ADR.
- **Gate.** `poetry run ruff check src/ tests/ eval/ && poetry run ruff format --check src/ tests/ eval/ && poetry run mypy src/ && poetry run pytest -q -p no:cacheprovider --cov=wiki_studio --cov-fail-under=92` stays green; coverage does not fall below 92%; both evaluation runs' determinism tests stay green.
